Restricting the Weil quadratic form to a finite Fourier space on an interval of length \(L\) gives a Hermitian matrix. When its least eigenvalue \(E\) is simple with an even eigenvector, the construction of Connes, Consani and Moscovici attaches to that eigenvector an entire characteristic with only real zeros. We show that a uniform bound on one quantity supplies both compactness of these characteristics as \(L\to\infty\) and retention of the zeros of \(\Xi(z)=\xi(\tfrac12+iz)\). That quantity is the complete characteristic mean, a smoothed count of all zero pairs of the characteristic, including those of the untouched Fourier lattice. For finite Hermitian Fourier matrices with a rank-two displacement relation, a mean bound makes the energy above \(E\) of the ground state, smoothed by a sufficiently wide heat-difference filter, at most \(CL(H_{00}-E)e^{-cL^2}\), where \(H_{00}\) is the constant-mode diagonal entry. The estimate is uniform in the Fourier cutoff and needs no spectral-gap bound; its proof transfers every entire filter of the ground state to the constant mode. For the arithmetic matrices, \(H_{00}-E\) grows at most like \(Le^{L/2}\), and the mean bound forces the cutoff to grow at least like \(L^2\). Consequently, if the spectral conditions and a uniform mean bound hold along \(L=\log x\) for a sequence of integers \(x\to\infty\), the characteristics normalized at \(4i\) have nonzero entire subsequential limits with only real zeros, and every zero of \(\Xi\) is a zero of each limit. Such a sequence would imply the Riemann hypothesis. Its existence and the mean bound remain open, and nonarithmetic examples show that control of the constant mode alone cannot bound the mean.
