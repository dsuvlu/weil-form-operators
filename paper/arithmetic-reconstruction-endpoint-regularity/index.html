The θ-correlation companion reduces the sign of the positive-rate Weil kernel to one question. Does an explicit source \(b_*\) lie in the range of a compact Hankel operator \(H_b\)? Equivalently, is the unique square-integrable solution of a fixed convolution equation causal? We construct the causal solution \(q_c\) directly from arithmetic. With \(\kappa(t)=e^{-t}\) for \(t>0\), the response \(u=q_c+\kappa\) has Laplace transform \(U(r)=\dfrac{2\,\xi(\tfrac12+r)}{(r+1)(\xi+\xi')(\tfrac12+r)}\). A weighted Volterra equation produces it on positive times, and at each time \(t\) it is a finite sum over integer delays \(\log n\le t\) with von Mangoldt convolution coefficients. On every finite interval, finite Möbius inversion reconstructs it from its completed output, and conjugation to the Euler edge leaves one fixed forcing equation. Unconditionally, the edge response \(g=e^{-t/2}u\) satisfies \(\|t^k g\|\le AB^k(k!)^2\), so \(e^{a\sqrt t}\,g\in L^2\) for some \(a>0\). The source \(b_*\) lies in the range of \(|H_b|^{1-\delta}\) for every \(0<\delta<1\), and of no \(|H_b|^{1+\delta}\) with \(\delta>0\). Its regularized inverse energy is at most \(C[1+\log(1/\lambda)]^{1/2}\), with a Gevrey refinement. These bounds leave the first power undecided. Conditionally, if \(U\) has no genuine pole in a half-plane \(\operatorname{Re} r>a\) with \(0\le a<\tfrac12\), then \(e^{\beta t}g\in L^2\) for an explicit \(\beta>0\); poles cancelled by zeros of \(\xi\) do not count. Finally, the Riemann hypothesis is equivalent to the absence of genuine poles in \(\operatorname{Re} r>0\), to \(e^{\beta t}g\in L^2\) for every \(0<\beta<\tfrac12\), and to \(q_c\in L^2(0,\infty)\), which is the range condition \(b_*\in\operatorname{Ran}H_b\) itself.
