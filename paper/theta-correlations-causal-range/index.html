The completed-operator construction of the Weil form yields a response kernel on positive rates, \(K(r,s)=\dfrac{\ell(r)+\ell(s)}{r+s}\), where \(\ell=M'/M\) and \(M(r)=\xi(\tfrac12+r)/\xi(\tfrac12)\). Its values are limits of localized Weil pairings on completed outputs of exponential sources. We ask whether every finite matrix of this kernel is positive semidefinite. That condition is much stronger than the positivity of \(\ell'\) at each rate. The classical θ probability law has moment generating function \(M\). Using it, we realize \(K\) as the correlation kernel of an explicit self-adjoint Hilbert–Schmidt Markov operator, so positivity of all finite matrices is positivity of this one operator. Through a derivative-trace criterion and a one-sided support criterion for a completed Cayley unitary, we show that this is also equivalent to membership of one explicit source \(b_*\) in the range of a compact self-adjoint Hankel operator \(H_b\). That range is dense but not closed, so an actual preimage is required. When one exists, it is unique and causal, with squared norm \(\tfrac12\). Accordingly, the regularized inverse energy of \(b_*\) tends to \(\tfrac12\) when \(b_*\in\operatorname{Ran}H_b\) and diverges otherwise. In the second case a rank-one perturbation of \(H_b^2\) has exactly one negative eigenvalue. Replacing the θ law by an explicit positive, even density gives positive coupling entries and a log-density concave in the squared variable, yet the matrix sign fails. By the arithmetic companion, \(b_*\in\operatorname{Ran}H_b\) is equivalent to the Riemann hypothesis, and so therefore is positivity of all finite matrices of \(K\). Neither is decided here.
