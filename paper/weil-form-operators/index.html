The Weil explicit formula expresses a Hermitian pairing through prime powers and the Γ and polar terms of the completed ζ function; restricting it to a finite interval gives the localized Weil form. We represent this form as the boundary response of a completed arithmetic operator family. On the full line, the functional equation makes the squared output norm of each fixed source stationary at the critical parameter \(s=\tfrac12\). For a source supported in a fixed interval, we prove that the Weil form on its retained completed output is the negative derivative, evaluated at \(s=\tfrac12\), of the energy escaping the interval under real variation of \(s\). The same pairing is half of a compressed difference of two orthogonal projections, \(P_- - \mathcal{U}P_-\mathcal{U}^*\), for a unitary \(\mathcal{U}\) constructed from the completed family.

The family is built from one-sided translations in logarithmic coordinates, which give finite Euler and Möbius identities on bounded windows. The uncompleted critical half-line operator is not closable. Adjoining the normalized Γ and polar factors, however, yields a bounded convolution operator whose explicit kernel is a weighted Müntz transform of a mean-zero polynomial. We construct a holomorphic family near the critical parameter. We then show that the critical source-Gram derivative on the half-line is trace class and is the trace-norm limit of its finite support compressions.

We also derive the complete localized Fourier matrix: the Γ multiplier on the diagonal plus divided differences of one explicit real odd function. When the full least eigenvalue is simple and its eigenvector is even, we recover the Connes–Consani–Moscovici finite characteristic, with only real zeros, as the Fourier transform of the ground state. The completed finite-window range consists precisely of \(H^1\) functions vanishing at the terminal endpoint, and its projection onto every finite Fourier section is surjective. Positivity of the form and the limits of the characteristics as the interval grows are studied in companion papers.

Parts of the finite arithmetic and finite-window results are formalized in Lean 4; see the theorem map for exactly which statements are machine-checked.
